# SeasonSafe: extended analyses (post-hoc)

Added 19 November 2026. **Every analysis below was designed after the locked held-out results in
FINDINGS.md had been seen.** None of it replaces the primary result. It explains the primary
result, stress-tests it and narrows what can be claimed. Outputs: `results_extended/`, generated by
`python extended_analysis.py`. Every CSV carries an `analysis_status` column marking it post-hoc.

Reproduction check: re-running `experiment.py` on `results/source_dataset.csv` with the pinned versions
in `requirements_verified.txt` (Python 3.13) matched every published primary and sensitivity CSV to
within 1e-12. `extended_analysis.py` refuses to run if its refitted cutoff differs from the locked one.

## Summary: what changes in the story

1. **Two of the eight "arrival-context" features carry no information at evaluation time.**
   Training covers Feb–Sep, so the Month categories Oct, Nov and Dec never appear in training. The
   one-hot encoder therefore writes all zeros for Month on every validation and test row, a pattern
   no training row had. SpecialDay is non-zero only in Feb (79 rows) and May (1,172 rows); it is 0 for
   every Oct–Dec session. Permutation importance confirms both features contribute exactly 0 in Oct–Dec.
2. **The size and direction of the capacity error depend on that Month artefact.** Dropping Month
   (same algorithm, same protocol) changes the frozen-cutoff selections from 249→232 in Nov and
   182→139 in Dec for boosting; for logistic regression, 236→143 and 183→105. December's overflow
   (the headline example of exceeding capacity) becomes an underflow in every ablation variant.
3. **The cutoff's own estimation noise is large.** Bootstrapping the 549 October sessions gives a
   95% interval of 199–301 selected sessions in November (budget 299) and 127–208 in December
   (budget 172). November under-fills in 96% of resamples. December over-fills in only 80% of them,
   so even before the ablations, the December overflow was not a firm finding.
4. **The model is close to a "new visitors first" rule.** The frozen cutoff selects 40–51% of new
   visitors and about 1% of returning visitors in every month. The rule "new visitors first, random within
   group" reaches 30.4% top-10% precision in Nov (tie-break range 28.4–33.1%) and 23.8% in Dec
   (18.0–26.7%); the model reaches 32.8% and 23.3%. A rule based on the training purchase rate by
   TrafficType beats the model in Nov (34.1%) but not in Dec (16.9%). The ML adds little that a
   manager could not write by hand.
5. **The cutoff drifts because the traffic mix shifted.** TrafficType has by far the largest
   population-stability index (PSI). Types 4 and 5 (17% of October traffic) almost vanish in
   Nov–Dec, while types 8 and 10 (absent in October) appear at about 12%. New-visitor share moves
   from 22.6% (Oct) to 14.0% (Nov) to 19.4% (Dec); because the model selects mainly new visitors,
   that share largely sets how many sessions clear the cutoff.

The capacity-control conclusion survives: a frozen score cutoff does not hold a workload steady
across months. What does **not** survive is any specific claim about the size or direction of the
error, and any claim that the arrival-context model adds much beyond VisitorType and TrafficType.

## Details

### A. Ablation without Month / SpecialDay (`ablation_month_specialday.csv`)
Boosting (hist_boost), 10% budget:

| Feature set | Oct AP | Nov selected / 299 | Dec selected / 172 | Nov AP | Dec AP |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Arrival context (primary) | 0.2502 | 249 | 182 | 0.3209 | 0.1944 |
| minus Month | 0.2524 | 232 | 139 | 0.3213 | 0.1995 |
| minus Month and SpecialDay | 0.2422 | 215 | 128 | 0.3232 | 0.1989 |
| VisitorType only | 0.2134 | 419 | 335 | 0.2630 | 0.1575 |

Ranking quality (AP, ROC-AUC) is essentially unchanged without Month; only the score *level*, and so
the cutoff's behaviour, changes. VisitorType alone produces massive ties (two distinct scores), so a
cutoff on it cannot control capacity at all.

### B. Rule baselines (`rule_baselines.csv`, `rule_new_visitor_tie_spread.csv`, Fig. 06)

| Policy, top 10% | Oct | Nov | Dec |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Primary model | 20.4% | 32.8% | 23.3% |
| New visitors first (seed 161) | 14.8% | 30.4% | 23.8% |
| Training rate by TrafficType | 14.8% | 34.1% | 16.9% |
| Training rate by VisitorType × TrafficType | 16.7% | 32.4% | 22.1% |
| Random (expected = base rate) | 20.9% | 25.4% | 12.5% |

### C. Uncertainty of the capacity finding (`cutoff_uncertainty.csv`)
2,000 bootstrap resamples of October; the model is held fixed, so model-refit uncertainty is not included.

| Month | Budget | Point | 95% interval | Share of draws under / over budget |
|---|---:|---:|---|---|
| Nov | 299 | 249 | 199–301 | 95.9% / 4.2% |
| Dec | 172 | 182 | 127–208 | 3.3% / 79.7% |

### D. Seed and model-choice sensitivity (`seed_sensitivity.csv`, `temporal_cv_*.csv`)
- Seeds 161–170: boosting wins every time. It has no subsampling, so it is deterministic. The random
  forest varies (October AP 0.233–0.238) but never wins. The seed is not a source of fragility.
- The real fragility is algorithm choice. Logistic regression lost by 0.0014 October AP; had it won, Nov
  and Dec selections would have been 236 and 183.
- Training-only expanding-window tuning (validate on Jun, Jul, Aug, Sep using earlier months; small grids)
  picks C=0.01 for logistic, min_samples_leaf=25 for RF and similar settings for boosting. Tuned models
  give October AP 0.234–0.248 (no gain over the untuned primary) and frozen-cutoff selections of 175–229
  (Nov) and 162–167 (Dec). Tuning does not remove capacity drift.

### E. Recalibration (`recalibration.csv`)
Platt and isotonic maps fitted on October predictions only:

| Month | Calibration | Brier | Predicted / actual buyers in top 10% |
|---|---|---:|---|
| Nov | none | 0.1970 | 100.5 / 98 |
| Nov | Platt (Oct) | 0.1874 | 88.8 / 98 |
| Dec | none | 0.1089 | 58.6 / 40 |
| Dec | Platt (Oct) | 0.1138 | 51.4 / 40 |

Recalibrating on October improves November and worsens December. October's base rate (20.9%) sits
between Nov (25.4%) and Dec (12.5%), so no single static correction fits both months. Calibration needs
monitoring with recent labels, not a one-off fix.

### F. Drift diagnostics (`drift_psi.csv`, `selection_by_visitor_type.csv`, Fig. 07)
PSI against October: TrafficType 2.12 (Nov), 3.93 (Dec); VisitorType 0.13, 0.44; Browser 0.06, 0.47;
OS 0.12, 0.45; Region and Weekend < 0.03. These PSI values use a 1e-4 floor for categories with zero
share in one month, which inflates PSI when a category appears or disappears. Read them as a ranking of
which features moved, not as calibrated magnitudes.

### G. Permutation importance (`permutation_importance.csv`, Fig. 05)
AP drop when shuffled (30 repeats): TrafficType 0.024/0.039/0.021 (Oct/Nov/Dec), VisitorType
−0.015/0.015/0.026, OS 0.008/0.020/0.010, Browser < 0.01. Month and SpecialDay: exactly 0.

### H. Label-free rolling cutoff (`rolling_cutoff_policy.csv`)
A deployable middle policy: each month's cutoff is the previous month's 10% score quantile, using
scores only (no purchase labels). November is identical to the frozen policy. In December it selected
205 against 172 (33 over budget), worse than the frozen October cutoff (182). A one-month-lagged
quantile does not track a mix shift that reverses between months. This is a negative result from a
single informative month.

### I. Capacity curve across budgets (`capacity_curve.csv`, Fig. 04)
For targets from 2% to 30%, the October cutoff under-selects in November at every target from 6%
upward (it over-selects at 2–4%). In December it over-selects up to a 10% target and under-selects from 12%. So the direction of December's
error also depends on the budget.

### J. Capacity-mismatch cost scenarios (`capacity_cost_scenarios.csv`)
Cost in idle-slot units = unused slots + r × overflow sessions, for an **assumed** ratio r ∈ {0.5, 1, 2, 4}.
November's frozen cutoff costs 50 units at any r. December's costs 5–40 units. The monthly ranking costs 0
by construction. There is no real cost data; this only shows that in this run, under-use in November
dominates unless overflow is valued above 5× an idle slot.

## Boundaries
All of the above is post-hoc and conditional on one year of one retailer's data. Ablations, tuning and
recalibration were chosen with knowledge of the test results, so they must not be presented as
confirmatory evidence or used to pick a "better" primary model.
